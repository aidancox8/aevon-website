How to book follow-up appointments from an OSCAR tickler

Short answer. A tickler never books anything on its own. Someone has to contact the patient, agree on a time and put the visit on the schedule. Clinics do that in five ways: by phone, from a recall list, with a booking link, with reminders, or with a text conversation that books. Only the last one works the tickler without a person picking up the phone.

What a tickler holds

A tickler in OSCAR is a dated task attached to a patient. It has a Service Date, a Priority of High, Normal or Low, one person it is assigned to under Assign Task To, and a free-text message. Its status is Active, Completed or Deleted.

A doctor writes something like "Follow-up in 6 weeks, 15 min" and assigns it to the front desk. The timeframe and the visit length are only words in the message. OSCAR does not turn them into an appointment.

1. By phone

Staff open the Tickler screen, read the message, phone the patient, check the schedule while talking and book the visit. Then they tick the tickler and use Complete Selected.

A person on the phone can handle anything: a longer visit, a family member booking for someone else, a request that does not match the tickler's wording. The cost is time. It is one call at a time, often several tries per patient, and a tickler only gets worked on the days someone opens the list.

2. From a recall or preventions list

OSCAR's Preventions module can hold a next due date, and its reports list the patients who are due. The front desk works down the list.

This is already in the EMR, so it costs nothing extra. But a list contacts nobody and books nothing, so it is really option 1 with a better starting point. It suits preventive care with a clear due date. It suits a follow-up ordered for a specific reason less well, because the tickler carries the reason and the list does not.

3. A booking link sent to the patient

Online booking tools send the patient a link to a page where they pick a time, and the booking is written back to the schedule.

The patient can book outside office hours and nobody re-keys anything. The weakness is that a link is not a conversation. If the patient does not click, nothing happens and the tickler stays open until staff notice. Someone also has to send the link for each tickler unless the tool does that itself.

4. Reminders only

Reminder tools message patients ahead of a visit that is already booked, so fewer of them miss it.

That protects booked visits. It does not book a follow-up from a tickler, because there is no appointment yet to remind anyone about. A clinic with a reminder tool still needs a person, or another tool, to turn the tickler into a visit first.

5. A text conversation that books

Here the doctor assigns the tickler to a tool in place of a person. The tool reads the message, finds open times on the doctor's schedule and texts the patient three of them. The patient replies 1, 2 or 3. The tool books that time, confirms it, and sets the tickler to Completed with a note such as "Booked Wed 30 Dec 13:15 by text".

Follow-up booking from Aevon Health works this way, by text or email, never by voice. A patient who asks for other times gets new ones. A patient who does not reply gets two reminders, then the tickler goes back to the front desk with a note. Anything clinical, urgent or unclear goes to the front desk with the patient's own words quoted. Texts go only to patients who have agreed to texts from the clinic. It connects through OSCAR's official API, using one account your clinic controls.

Which one fits

  • A small follow-up load: stay with the phone, and use the preventions list to find patients who are due.
  • Patients who already book online: a booking link uses a habit they have.
  • Booked visits lost to no-shows: reminders solve that problem first.
  • Follow-ups piling up as open ticklers: a text conversation is the only route where the tickler gets worked without a phone call.

Questions

Does an OSCAR tickler book the appointment?

No. A tickler is a dated task attached to a patient. It reminds someone that a visit is needed. A person, or a tool connected to OSCAR, still has to contact the patient and put the visit on the schedule.

Does the tickler close itself once the visit is booked?

No. After booking, staff open the Tickler screen, tick the tickler and use Complete Selected, which sets its status to Completed. If nobody does this, it stays Active.

Can a tickler be assigned to software in place of a person?

A tickler is assigned to a provider account. Aevon Health uses its own account in your OSCAR, so a doctor picks it from the Assign Task To list the same way they would pick a staff member.

What happens if the patient never replies to a text?

With Aevon Health they get two reminders. After that the tickler goes back to your front desk with a note saying there was no reply.

Is this the same in OSCAR Pro and Juno?

The idea is the same, but screens and names can differ. Check your own version.
