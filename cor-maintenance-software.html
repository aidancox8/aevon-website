Software to maintain COR certification

COR is audited every year, but nobody fails an audit in the audit. They fail in the eleven months before it: a ticket lapses, a new hire's orientation never gets written down, a leaver stays on the list. Maintaining COR is a records job, and records are what software is for. This page has a free expiry calculator and what maintenance software should actually do.

What slips between audits

  • Expiries nobody booked. The most common records failure: the ticket lapsed before anyone noticed, because the only reminder was the date on the card in someone's wallet.
  • New hires with no record. The person started, did the orientation, and nobody wrote it down. At audit time there is no evidence it happened.
  • Leavers still on the list. The crew list says 40 people and payroll says 34. An auditor notices the gap before you do.
  • Renewals that were never filed. The course was taken and the new card exists, but the record still shows the old expiry.

Four failure modes, and every one of them is a system problem, not a people problem. That is why a checklist helps once and software helps all year.

    What the software should do

    If you are comparing options for keeping COR records current, these are the five things that matter. In that order.

    1. Every certificate carries a real expiry date. Issue date, renewal period, expiry, per person and per ticket. Not a text field somebody types "current" into.
    2. It reminds people before the date, not after. The worker first, then their supervisor, with enough time left to book the course.
    3. The roster matches reality. New hires appear, leavers are marked as left, and nobody has to retype the staff list into a second system. Cadre syncs the roster from the HR or payroll system you already run.
    4. Renewing closes the loop. The training record and the certification are the same record, so completing the course clears the reminder instead of creating a second entry to reconcile.
    5. An auditor gets an export, not an afternoon. Who is current, filtered by site, ticket or status, as a CSV, on demand.

    Questions

    Is there software that maintains COR certification?

    Software cannot certify you; an auditor does that. What software can do is keep the training and certification records behind COR current all year, so the audit is a review rather than a scramble. That is the records side this page covers.

    Does records software replace the COR auditor?

    No. The audit itself is done by a certified auditor against the BCCSA audit tool. Records software makes sure the evidence the auditor asks for exists and is easy to hand over.

    What records does COR maintenance come down to?

    Which tickets each role needs, the issue and expiry date of every certificate, renewals booked before expiry, orientation records for new hires, and records kept for workers who have left. If those five are current, the records side of an audit is straightforward.

    How is this different from the COR readiness checklist?

    The readiness checklist is a point-in-time check of where your records stand today. Maintenance software keeps them current continuously: expiry dates tracked, renewals reminded, leavers handled, exports ready on demand.
