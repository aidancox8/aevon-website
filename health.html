Your staff already know what falls through.

The work after a fax is sorted is done by hand today, and it piles up.

The pile that never shrinks

Sorting a fax takes seconds. Finishing it takes a person: confirm, chase, book, confirm again. So the pile grows.

The referral that arrives twice

When a referral sits, the referring office sends it again and the patient phones to ask. Both land on the same pile.

The slot that stayed empty

A patient cancels a day or two ahead. Filling the slot means phoning down the waitlist, and there is rarely time for that, so the appointment goes unused.

How it works

  1. The fax gets sorted

    By your EMR's own tools if it has them. If it does not, we can sort and label incoming faxes too. Either way we connect through the EMR's official API, using one account your clinic controls.

  2. The automation finishes the routine parts

    It checks the referral against your list, asks for what is missing, and offers the patient times you allow.

  3. Routine referrals book themselves

    When a referral meets every rule your clinic sets, it is booked and confirmed with nobody at your clinic touching it. Anything that does not is handed to a person with the reason. The automation does not guess, and it never decides urgency.

Two jobs, done properly.

Referral intake: from fax to booked

A new referral goes from a sorted fax to a booked appointment. Routine ones need no work from your staff or physicians.

  • Acknowledges the referring office when the referral arrives
  • Asks the patient or the referring office for what is missing
  • Offers the patient times by text and books the one they pick
  • Sends the confirmation and welcome email
  • Hands exceptions to your staff with the reason, as tasks in your EMR
  • Staff confirmation before booking is available if your clinic wants it
  • Sorts and labels incoming faxes too, if nothing does that for you today

Questions clinics ask

Is it available now?

Not yet. Both automations are in development. We are looking for founding clinics to build them with, starting with clinics that use OSCAR.

Does this replace our EMR or booking tool?

No. It works alongside your EMR and does not replace it. If you use an online booking tool, that keeps working the way it does now.

Does it replace the tool that sorts our faxes?

No. If your EMR or an add-on already sorts and labels your faxes, it starts after that. If nothing does, it can sort them as well, with your staff confirming the labels until you are comfortable letting it run.

Does it decide which referrals are urgent?

No. It reads what the referring office wrote and follows the rules your clinic sets for provider and appointment type. Urgency is a clinical decision and stays with your physicians.

How does a new referral get booked?

The patient is offered times your clinic allows, by text. When they pick one, a referral that meets all your clinic's rules is booked and the confirmation and welcome email go out. If anything is missing or unusual, it goes to your staff instead. Your clinic can also choose to confirm every booking.

What happens when it is not sure?

It stops and hands the referral to a person, with the reason: a missing phone number, two patients with the same name, a referral marked urgent by the sender, or anything outside your clinic's rules. It does not guess.

Do physicians have to review each referral first?

Only if your clinic wants that. Many clinics have a physician accept or prioritise new referrals. That step stays with the physician, and the automation waits for it. Where your clinic's rules allow a referral to be booked directly, it is.

Do staff need to learn new software?

No. Exceptions show up as tasks inside your EMR.

Do patients get messages from it?

Yes, for booking: a short form request, the offered times and the confirmation. The messages contain no health details.

Where is patient information stored?

In Canada, and we store as little as possible.

What does it cost?

Pricing is per physician per month, plus a one-time setup fee. Founding clinics start with a 60-day pilot at no charge.

What if we want to stop?

Your clinic disables the one EMR account we use, and our access ends.

How does it fill a cancelled appointment?

When an appointment is cancelled in your EMR, patients on your own waitlist who fit that provider and time get a text offering the slot. The first to reply YES is booked. Your front desk can approve each booking first.

Which EMRs does it work with?

We are building for OSCAR first. Accuro and TELUS CHR are planned. It connects through the EMR's official API, so each EMR is its own piece of work.

How does it fit with privacy law in BC and Ontario?

Your clinic stays in control of the patient record. We act as your service provider under BC's PIPA, or as your agent under Ontario's PHIPA, and we prepare a privacy impact assessment for your privacy officer before anything goes live.

Do we still need our fax service or booking tool?

Yes. Nothing changes there. Referrals still go out the way they do today, and your online booking tool keeps working the way it does now.

Is this AI making decisions about patients?

No. The automations follow rules that your clinic sets, and they only handle administrative follow-up. They do no triage, diagnosis or treatment. Clinical decisions stay with your physicians. Where it reads a fax to sort it, it uses a language model run in Canada under a no-retention agreement, and your clinic's data is never used to train it.
