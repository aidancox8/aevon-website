Referral acknowledgement in BC: what the College guideline says

Short answer. The College of Physicians and Surgeons of BC sets no number of days for acknowledging a referral. Its guideline recommends telling the referring office the referral arrived as soon as possible, then accepting or declining it within about two weeks. A letter you can copy is further down this page.

The three time points

These come from the College's professional guideline Referral-Consultation Process, version 5.4, revised June 10, 2026.

StepWhat the guideline asks of the consulting officeTiming
Referral receivedSend a notice that the referral has arrived. The guideline calls this especially helpful where the wait to be seen is long.As soon as possible. No number of days is given.
Accepted or notSend an informative response that accepts or declines the referral. If declined, give the reason and suggest alternatives where appropriate.Promptly. In the guideline's words, "two weeks is considered prompt".
Consultation reportSend a written report after seeing the patient, and phone if the results are urgent or critical.Within two weeks, which the guideline attributes to the MSP fee guide, except in extraordinary circumstances.

Guideline or requirement

It is a professional guideline. The College describes a guideline as a recommended course of action and says physicians may use reasonable discretion in applying it. It is not written as a rule with a penalty attached.

Two points in it still matter to an office manager. The College says that in a complaint it would look at the conduct of both the referring and the consulting physician. It also says the patient should know exactly how they will hear about the appointment, and that both offices share the job of making sure they do.

Who tells the patient

  • The consulting office, in most cases. The guideline says it is best placed to give the date and time to both the referring office and the patient, along with any preparation the patient needs.
  • The referring office stays responsible while the patient waits. If the patient's condition changes before the appointment, the referring physician provides the care and should tell the patient so.
  • Both offices get told at the end. When the consultation is complete and care goes back to the referring physician, the patient and the referring office should both be notified.

A referral received letter you can copy

This follows the points in the College's own sample (Appendix B of the guideline) in plainer wording. Fill in the blanks, delete the lines you do not need, and fax it back the day the referral arrives.

REFERRAL RECEIVED To: Dr. ____________________ Fax: ____________________ From: ____________________ (clinic) Phone: ____________________ Fax: ____________________ Date received: ____________________ We have received your referral for: Patient: ____________________ Date of birth: ____________________ Dr. ____________________ will review the referral and triage it by urgency. What happens next [ ] If the referral is accepted, our office will contact the patient with the appointment date, time and any preparation needed. We will also send the appointment details to your office. [ ] If the referral cannot be accepted, we will tell you why and suggest an alternative where we can. You will hear from us within two weeks of the date above. Still needed before we can book [ ] ____________________ [ ] ____________________ [ ] ____________________ Please fax these to the number above. Our usual wait for a first appointment is ____________________. Languages spoken by the physician: ____________________. Please let us know if the patient will need an interpreter. Please do not pass this notice to the patient as an appointment confirmation. We will confirm with the patient directly once a time is booked. If the patient's condition changes while they wait, please send us an update marked urgent. ____________________ (name and role)

Making it routine

  1. Acknowledge the same day the fax is opened. The notice does not depend on the physician's decision, so it does not need to wait for triage.
  2. Write the two-week date on the referral. That is the day the accept or decline response is due.
  3. List what is missing in the first notice. Asking once, on day one, saves a second round of faxes later.
  4. Keep one list of referrals waiting for a decision. Check it on a fixed day each week so nothing passes two weeks unanswered.

Questions

How long does a specialist in BC have to acknowledge a referral?

The guideline gives no number of days for the acknowledgement. It recommends sending it as soon as possible. The response that accepts or declines the referral is the step with a time attached, and two weeks is treated as prompt.

Is acknowledging a referral mandatory in BC?

The document is a professional guideline, which the College describes as a recommended course of action. The College also says that in a complaint it would consider the conduct of both the referring and the consulting physician.

Who tells the patient about the specialist appointment?

Usually the consulting office. The guideline also says both offices share the obligation to make sure the patient knows the appointment details.

How soon is the consultation report due?

The guideline asks for a timely written report and notes that the MSP fee guide requires it within two weeks of seeing the patient, except in extraordinary circumstances.

Does this apply to referrals from nurse practitioners?

The guideline says physicians should follow it, as appropriate, when working with other health-care providers such as nurse practitioners and pharmacists.
